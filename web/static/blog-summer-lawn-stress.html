How to Keep Your Lawn Healthy Through Louisiana Summer Heat

A practical routine for warm-season grass in LaPlace, Reserve, and the River Parishes.

July 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Summer heat does not automatically mean a lawn is failing. Warm-season grasses can handle Louisiana temperatures when mowing, watering, and soil conditions work together. The goal is steady growth, not a bright green lawn at any cost.

Mow high and keep the blade sharp

Set the mower high enough to leave useful leaf area, and sharpen the blade regularly. A dull blade tears the grass and increases water loss. Never remove more than one-third of the blade height in a single cut.

Water deeply, not constantly

Water early in the morning so moisture reaches the root zone before the hottest part of the day. Check the soil beneath the surface instead of watering by the clock. Short daily sprinkling encourages shallow roots and can keep foliage wet overnight.

Simple test: Place a straight-sided container in the irrigation area. Measure the water collected and move the container around the yard to find dry spots and overspray.

Watch for heat stress signals

  • Footprints remain visible after walking across the lawn.
  • Blades curl, lose color, or wilt during the afternoon.
  • Soil pulls away from hard edges or becomes difficult to penetrate.
  • Some areas stay wet while others dry quickly, pointing to drainage or coverage problems.

Do not solve every brown spot with fertilizer

Brown grass can come from drought, insects, disease, compacted soil, shade, or poor drainage. Fertilizer will not correct all of those causes. Identify the pattern first, then choose a treatment that matches the problem.

Make shade and traffic part of the plan

Grass under dense tree cover or in a heavily used path may never perform like open turf. Thin those areas with pruning, a defined walkway, groundcover, or mulch rather than repeatedly forcing grass to grow where conditions are unsuitable.
